# Datasheet: When the Network Goes Dark (data and code)

Version 4.0.0. Following the sections of Gebru et al. (2021), "Datasheets for Datasets" (Communications of the ACM 64(12)). Generated by `code/src/14_data_repo.py` from the README, `CODEBOOK.md`, `SOURCES.md`, `docs/decisions.md` and `ERRATA.md`; every number comes from `data/analysis/results.json` or the data, so it changes when they do.

## Motivation

- **Purpose.** To put the FCC's daily Disaster Information Reporting System (DIRS) cell-site reports for Gulf and Southeast hurricanes side by side, checked line by line, so that how much of the network goes dark, how fast it comes back, what is reported to take it down and who waits longest can be described across storms. The FCC publishes the reports one PDF at a time, storm by storm, in layouts that change between years.

## Composition

- **Instances.** One row per county per FCC DIRS report: 11,296 county-report rows (`data/analysis/dirs_county_day.csv`) from 174 reports for 22 storms, 916 counties in ten states, reports dated 2017-08-26 to 2026-10-12. `storms.csv` has one row per storm in scope (22, including any with no usable report).
- **Fields.** Cell sites served and out, sites out by cause (damage, transport, power) where the FCC printed them, sites on backup power where printed, and the report's validation status. The analysis-ready panel (`panel_analysis_ready.csv` and `.parquet`) has the same 11,296 rows and 79 columns, adding power outages, exposure, day 0, SVI, ACS and broadband provider counts. Every variable: `CODEBOOK.md`.
- **Is it a sample?** No: it covers every activation the inclusion rule admits, 2017-2026, and every one of their reports. **Inclusion rule.** A DIRS activation is included when it is for an Atlantic named storm, hurricane or tropical storm (a storm that never reaches hurricane strength is included too; every storm in this release did, at some point in its track, per HURDAT2), its activation date is in 2017-2026, and its public Communications Status Reports print county rows in at least one of the ten study states (Texas, Louisiana, Mississippi, Alabama, Florida, Georgia, South Carolina, North Carolina, Tennessee, Virginia). Within an included storm, a report whose rows all lie outside those states is kept in the manifest but not used, and rows outside them are dropped. The series starts in 2017 because the committed analysis plan (2026-11-22) fixed the scope at 2017-2026, the years whose report layouts the parser was built and checked on; the only 2016 activation, Matthew, has not been read and is planned as a separate module (docs/roadmap_modules.md). Other hazards (earthquakes, wildfires, winter storms, power shutoffs) and the territories are outside the rule. This dataset is the Gulf and Southeast hurricane subset of a larger public DIRS record: the FCC has also published status reports for activations in the territories and for other hazards (docs/dirs_activations.csv). The inventory of activations known here, with each one's dates, geography, reports, table columns and decision, is `docs/dirs_activations.csv`; later extensions are planned as separate modules (`docs/roadmap_modules.md`).
- **Report counts.** From the manifest to the reports used (none excluded):

- **Missing values.** Empty cells. Cause columns are missing where the FCC did not print them (below); backup power likewise; power outages are missing where EAGLE-I has no record or no release (below).
- **People.** None. The data are county aggregates of cell sites; there are no individuals and no personal data.
- **Providers.** The published files name no providers. Provider counts in `v2_5_groups` and `v2_5_network_redundancy` come from the FCC's Broadband Data Collection; raw BDC data is not redistributed.
- **Confidential data.** None: every input is public (`SOURCES.md`).

## Collection process

- **DIRS reports.** The FCC's Communications Status Reports (text file and PDF) for each storm, listed by hand from the FCC's storm pages (linked from its Past Response Efforts page) and downloaded from docs.fcc.gov on 2026-11-22, one request at a time, each saved unmodified with its URL, retrieval time and SHA-256 (`data/input/INPUTS.csv`). The FCC's DIRS notices give each storm's reporting area.
- **Other sources.** ORNL/DOE EAGLE-I power outages (figshare version 4), NOAA NHC HURDAT2 and best track, CDC/ATSDR SVI 2022, Census ACS 2022 and county files, OpenFEMA declarations and FCC Broadband Data Collection provider counts; each with its terms and access date in `SOURCES.md`.
- **Who collected it.** The author, with code written with the help of an AI coding assistant (`AI_ASSISTANCE.md`); every decision is logged with its date and reason in `docs/decisions.md`.
- **Timeframe.** Reports from 2017-08-26 to 2026-10-12.
- **Analysis plan.** The analysis plan was committed on November 22, 2026, in the project's private version-controlled repository (commit 9fc17a4), before any recovery outcome was computed. The plan file as committed is published in the data repository with its SHA-256, so it can be checked against later changes. It is not blind to some figures, which the plan lists. Every deviation from it is in `DEVIATIONS.md`.

## Preprocessing and cleaning

- Each report is parsed from its text file or, when that is incomplete, its PDF; county rows must sum to the report's printed state and overall totals, or the report is flagged or excluded with its reason. Every county row is checked against a second reading: in 154 reports, the PDF's plain text, which is independent of the parse (the parse used the .txt or the PDF's table cells); in the 24 reports whose parse itself used plain PDF text, where that reading would not be independent, the .txt (20) or PDFium's text (4) instead. `validation/validation_dirs.csv` names the reading for each report (`second_reading`). Counties are matched to FIPS codes with a checked exception list.
- Validation of the reports in scope: 130 flagged, 44 ok, 4 known FCC inconsistencies (`data/analysis/validation/`). The known FCC inconsistencies are printed figures that disagree with the report's own rows or totals, each checked against the PDF and kept as printed, with the figure computed from the rows used.
- External check: the FCC's own 2017 season report gives 80 county or state figures for Harvey and Irma; 60 of the 70 comparable figures agree, and in the other 10 the season report differs from the daily reports themselves (`docs/external_validation.md`).
- External checks added in 3.2.0 (exploratory): the FCC's report on Hurricane Michael (May 2019) gives 12 figures in its text; 7 of the 8 that can be tested match or are consistent, 1 differs (M5a; explained in `docs/external_validation.md`), and 4 statements with no number are shown beside our series. Olukola et al. (2026) extracted Helene from the same reports independently: the peak (4,562 sites out, 28 September 2024) and sites in the reporting area (37,717, 12.1% out) match exactly. Their Irma peak (4,370 sites out, 11 September 2017) matches our report's rows, counting Puerto Rico and the U.S. Virgin Islands, which are outside the panel. FCC 21-99 cites Ida's report of August 31, 2021 for 699 sites out for power against 354 for damage and transport: those are the report's Louisiana totals, and our county rows sum to the same.
- Independent re-extraction (2026-11-27): a reviewer checked the published v3.2.1 panel with an extractor, scripts and sample of their own (pdftotext text, a seeded stratified sample), reporting 0 mismatches over its 10,051 rows. It is an independent code path and reviewer, AI-assisted, and not a human-keyed sample; a human-keyed sample is planned before the data paper. Its rerun against this version is pending (`docs/external_validation.md`).
- Context tables added in 3.2.0 (`data/analysis/context/`): cable and wireline subscribers out per report, FEMA Individual Assistance and NCEI Storm Events hazard by storm and county. Descriptive only, under addendum 4.x-1; the NCEI files themselves are not redistributed.
- Nothing is imputed. Raw inputs are kept unmodified in `data/input/`; `run_all.py` rebuilds every derived file from them, and a test checks the rebuild against the published files.
- Every change to a published number is listed, with before, after and why, in `ERRATA.md`.

## Uses

- **Intended.** Describing how much of the cell network the FCC's reports show out in hurricanes, how fast it is reported back, what causes the reports give, and how that co-moves with power outages, exposure and social vulnerability; reproducing or extending the page; studying the FCC's public record itself.

**Uses not supported:**

- **Not a measure of service availability or of people without service.** Each FCC report says: "The number of cell site outages in a specific area does not necessarily correspond to the availability of wireless service to consumers in that area."
- **Not carrier or provider performance.** The counts combine all providers that report; no provider can be seen or compared.
- **Not causal estimates.** Every result is an association: what happened together, not what caused what.
- **Not a hazard or impact measure for the cell-site results.** The 3.2.0 context tables (cable and wireline, FEMA Individual Assistance, NCEI hazard) are descriptive; no association with cell outages has been computed, and any such use needs its own committed plan.
- **Not proof that the FCC's 2024–2026 rule changes did or didn't work.** The public reports can't yet show whether the FCC's 2024–2026 rule changes coincided with any change in recovery: one rule period has no storm, another has only Isaias, and the sites-served counts shift within storms by as much as 31%.

**Known limitations:**

- **Zone-to-county mapping (context hazard table).** The NWS zone-to-county file of 16 April 2026 (bp16ap26.dbx) is used for every storm, because the files in force for earlier storms are no longer published. A check on zone names guards against renumbered zones (57 zone events set aside), but some zone-to-county mapping error may remain.
- **Reporting ends before recovery is complete.** Only 6 to 11 of 20 storms with a report after their peak reached 90% restored before their reports ended, and in all 22 storms the FCC's last report still showed sites out (1,887 in total).
- **Censoring.** A county that leaves the reports while still out is censored, not recovered: 505 of 2,375 storm-counties left the reports while out and 264 were still out at their storm's last report.
- **Changing denominators.** The FCC's reporting area, and so the sites served, changes during a storm; over the counties every report of a storm lists, sites served move by up to 31% within a storm. Report counts and percents together.
- **Cause columns missing before 2019.** Harvey, Irma, Nate, Florence and Michael (2017, 2018) print no cause columns; they are left out of every cause share, not counted as zero.
- **EAGLE-I coverage gaps.** EAGLE-I counts electricity meters, and its coverage varies: of the county-reports, 4,287 have coverage marked ok, 2,289 low coverage (the state's covered share below 0.75), 4,589 no published coverage (reports in 2017, 2023, 2024), and 131 no EAGLE-I release yet (Isaias).
- **The county bootstrap.** The county bootstrap (`chapter_3` to `chapter_6` within each storm; `chapter_8` and `v2_5_network_redundancy` across storm-counties) resamples counties as if they were independent. Counties hit by the same storm share its wind, flooding, power grid and networks, so their outcomes are correlated, and these intervals may be somewhat narrow. The storm bootstrap (medians and pooled shares across storms, `chapter_7`'s zone shares, `v2_3_power_cell_timing`) resamples whole storms, and the models in `chapter_7`, `chapter_8` and `v2_5_network_redundancy` use storm- or county-clustered intervals. This is a note on the method, not a change to any result. (Chapters are named by their `results.json` keys; the README maps them to the page's chapters.)
- **Flags.** Most reports carry at least one flag, most of them benign; `clean_rows(level="strict")` keeps 10 of 22 storms. Use it as a sensitivity check.

- **Versions.** major: an existing published value, or the meaning of a variable, changes; minor: rows, storms, files or variables are added and every existing published value is unchanged; patch: no value changes (bytes may change, for example a format or documentation fix, and the changelog says so). Each version is archived with its own DOI.
- **If maintenance stops.** Every released version stays archived on Zenodo and citable: the concept DOI (10.5281/zenodo.24477809) resolves to the latest version, and each version DOI to its own files.

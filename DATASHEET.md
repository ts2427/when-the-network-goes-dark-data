# Datasheet: When the Network Goes Dark (data and code)

Version 3.1.1. Following the sections of Gebru et al. (2021), "Datasheets for Datasets" (Communications of the ACM 64(12)). Generated by `code/src/14_data_repo.py` from the README, `CODEBOOK.md`, `SOURCES.md`, `docs/decisions.md` and `ERRATA.md`; every number comes from `data/analysis/results.json` or the data, so it changes when they do.

## Motivation

- **Purpose.** To put the FCC's daily Disaster Information Reporting System (DIRS) cell-site reports for Gulf and Southeast hurricanes side by side, checked line by line, so that how much of the network goes dark, how fast it comes back, what is reported to take it down and who waits longest can be described across storms. The FCC publishes the reports one PDF at a time, storm by storm, in layouts that change between years.
- **Created by** Timothy D. Spivey (ORCID 0009-0009-8990-4308), Threshold Data Sciences, for the page *When the Network Goes Dark* (https://thresholddatasciences.com/analysis/when-the-network-goes-dark/).
- **Funding and independence.** The author works in the telecommunications industry. This analysis was done independently, on personal time, using only publicly available government data. It uses no employer data or confidential information, and the views are the author's alone. The author has no financial interest in any FCC proceeding.

## Composition

- **Instances.** One row per county per FCC DIRS report: 10,051 county-report rows (`data/analysis/dirs_county_day.csv`) from 169 reports for 22 storms, 814 counties in ten states, reports dated 2017-08-26 to 2026-10-12. `storms.csv` has one row per storm in scope (22, including any with no usable report).
- **Fields.** Cell sites served and out, sites out by cause (damage, transport, power) where the FCC printed them, sites on backup power where printed, and the report's validation status. The analysis-ready panel (`panel_analysis_ready.csv` and `.parquet`) has the same 10,051 rows and 78 columns, adding power outages, exposure, day 0, SVI, ACS and broadband provider counts. Every variable: `CODEBOOK.md`.
- **Is it a sample?** No: it covers every Atlantic storm with a DIRS activation in the ten study states, 2017-2026, and every one of their reports. Of 178 reports in scope, 5 are excluded because they fail validation (Laura 2020-08-28; Laura 2020-08-29; Laura 2020-08-30; Helene 2024-10-03; Helene 2024-10-04) and 4 have county rows only outside the study states, leaving 169.
- **Missing values.** Empty cells. Cause columns are missing where the FCC did not print them (below); backup power likewise; power outages are missing where EAGLE-I has no record or no release (below).
- **People.** None. The data are county aggregates of cell sites; there are no individuals and no personal data. The FCC's tables combine all providers, so no provider is named or identifiable, and the broadband provider counts carry no provider name or ID.
- **Confidential data.** None: every input is public (`SOURCES.md`).

## Collection process

- **DIRS reports.** The FCC's Communications Status Reports (text file and PDF) for each storm, listed by hand from the FCC's storm pages (linked from its Past Response Efforts page) and downloaded from docs.fcc.gov on 2026-11-22, one request at a time, each saved unmodified with its URL, retrieval time and SHA-256 (`data/input/INPUTS.csv`). The FCC's DIRS notices give each storm's reporting area.
- **Other sources.** ORNL/DOE EAGLE-I power outages (figshare version 4), NOAA NHC HURDAT2 and best track, CDC/ATSDR SVI 2022, Census ACS 2022 and county files, OpenFEMA declarations and FCC Broadband Data Collection provider counts; each with its terms and access date in `SOURCES.md`.
- **Who collected it.** The author, with code written with the help of an AI coding assistant (`AI_ASSISTANCE.md`); every decision is logged with its date and reason in `docs/decisions.md`.
- **Timeframe.** Reports from 2017-08-26 to 2026-10-12; the analysis plan was committed on November 22, 2026, before any recovery outcome was computed (`docs/`).

## Preprocessing and cleaning

- Each report is parsed from its text file or, when that is incomplete, its PDF; every county row is checked against a second reading of the PDF; county rows must sum to the report's printed state and overall totals, or the report is flagged or excluded with its reason. Counties are matched to FIPS codes with a checked exception list.
- Validation of the reports in scope: 126 flagged, 44 ok, 5 excluded, 3 known FCC inconsistencies (`data/analysis/validation/`). The known FCC inconsistencies are printed figures that disagree with the report's own rows or totals, each checked against the PDF and kept as printed, with the figure computed from the rows used.
- External check: the FCC's own 2017 season report gives 80 county or state figures for Harvey and Irma; 60 of the 70 comparable figures agree, and in the other 10 the season report differs from the daily reports themselves (`docs/external_validation.md`).
- Nothing is imputed. Raw inputs are kept unmodified in `data/input/`; `run_all.py` rebuilds every derived file from them, and a test checks the rebuild against the published files.
- Every change to a published number is listed, with before, after and why, in `ERRATA.md`.

## Uses

- **Intended.** Describing how much of the cell network the FCC's reports show out in hurricanes, how fast it is reported back, what causes the reports give, and how that co-moves with power outages, exposure and social vulnerability; reproducing or extending the page; studying the FCC's public record itself.

**Uses not supported:**

- **Not a measure of service availability or of people without service.** Each FCC report says: "The number of cell site outages in a specific area does not necessarily correspond to the availability of wireless service to consumers in that area."
- **Not carrier or provider performance.** The counts combine all providers that report; no provider can be seen or compared.
- **Not causal estimates.** Every result is an association: what happened together, not what caused what.
- **Not proof that the 2024-2025 rules did or didn't work.** The public reports can't yet show whether the FCC's 2024–2025 rules coincided with any change in recovery: one rule period has no storm, another has only Isaias, and the sites-served counts shift within storms by as much as 31%.

**Known limitations:**

- **Reporting ends before recovery is complete.** Only 6 to 11 of 20 storms with a report after their peak reached 90% restored before their reports ended, and in all 22 storms the FCC's last report still showed sites out (1,887 in total).
- **Censoring.** A county that leaves the reports while still out is censored, not recovered: 474 of 2,158 storm-counties left the reports while out and 264 were still out at their storm's last report.
- **Changing denominators.** The FCC's reporting area, and so the sites served, changes during a storm; over the counties every report of a storm lists, sites served move by up to 31% within a storm. Report counts and percents together.
- **Cause columns missing before 2019.** Harvey, Irma, Nate, Florence and Michael (2017, 2018) print no cause columns; they are left out of every cause share, not counted as zero.
- **EAGLE-I coverage gaps.** EAGLE-I counts electricity meters, and its coverage varies: of the county-reports, 3,878 have coverage marked ok, 1,705 low coverage (the state's covered share below 0.75), 4,337 no published coverage (reports in 2017, 2023, 2024), and 131 no EAGLE-I release yet (Isaias).
- **The county bootstrap.** The county bootstrap (chapters 3 to 6 within each storm; chapters 8 and 13 across storm-counties) resamples counties as if they were independent. Counties hit by the same storm share its wind, flooding, power grid and networks, so their outcomes are correlated, and these intervals may be somewhat narrow. The storm bootstrap (medians and pooled shares across storms, chapter 7's zone shares, chapter 12) resamples whole storms, and the models in chapters 7, 8 and 13 use storm- or county-clustered intervals. This is a note on the method, not a change to any result. (Chapter numbers here are those of the `results.json` keys; the README maps them to the page's chapters.)
- **Flags.** Most reports carry at least one flag, most of them benign; `clean_rows(level="strict")` keeps 10 of 22 storms. Use it as a sensitivity check.

## Maintenance

- **Maintainer.** Timothy D. Spivey (ORCID 0009-0009-8990-4308).
- **Updates.** Once a year, after each hurricane season, when EAGLE-I's release covering that season is out (usually February to April); each season's analysis plan is committed before its reports are read (`docs/update_procedure.md`). Each report is archived as soon as the FCC publishes it.
- **Reporting an error.** The "Report a data error" issue form (storm, report DOC, county, what is wrong, evidence); see `CONTRIBUTING.md`.
- **Corrections.** Made in the code or a checked table, never by hand in a published file; logged in `docs/decisions.md`; a change to a published number is a new major version, archived with its own DOI and listed in `ERRATA.md` with before, after and why.
- **If maintenance stops.** Every released version stays archived on Zenodo and citable: the concept DOI (10.5281/zenodo.24477809) resolves to the latest version, and each version DOI to its own files.
- **Contributions.** Welcome through issues and pull requests (`CONTRIBUTING.md`); they are applied to the working project and the repository is regenerated.
